The 7th Workshop on Extremely Precise Radial Velocities

Campus MIL, Montréal, Canada • 13‑18 June 2027
Conference theme: Challenges in Extremely Precise RVs
Propose a Splinter Session Key Dates
Conference theme

Challenges in Extremely Precise RVs

EPRV7 is built around one central idea: detecting true Earth analogues requires extreme precision and accuracy, which can only be achieved by joining forces between experts in instrument building, data extraction, RV computation and stellar signal mitigation. We are therefore asking every participant, speaker, and splinter organizer to help us look at what is challenging, what is unresolved, and what is still standing between us and that goal.

Talks and splinters that encourage constructive discussions around open problems, comparison of differing methods and approaches, and novel or unorthodox solutions to outstanding puzzles are strongly encouraged.

Topics

  • New instrumentation: design, calibration, and stability
  • Data reduction, pipelines, and post-processing
  • Stellar and solar variability; mitigation methods
  • Statistical modelling and machine learning for RV
  • Time-series spectroscopy and telluric correction
  • Recent science results from high-resolution spectroscopy

Format

How the Week Works

Mornings from Monday to Thursday are devoted to plenary sessions. Afternoons feature two or three parallel splinter sessions, except Wednesday, which is left free ahead of the conference dinner. Teams wishing to hold additional working meetings can request a room on campus for Friday 18 June.

This meeting is designed for discussion of ongoing challenges in the field. We encourage speakers to highlight the limitations they encounter in their work and to foster constructive discussion that moves the community forward.

Further details on invited speakers and the abstract submission process will follow in a subsequent announcement.

Now open

Call for Splinter Sessions

We invite proposals for splinter sessions built around the conference theme, "Challenges in Extremely Precise RVs". Each proposal should include a short paragraph explaining how the session addresses a specific challenge, open problem, or unresolved tension in the field, rather than serving primarily as a venue for results presentations.

We welcome proposals that depart from the classic sequence of short talks, for example hands-on demos, data analysis get-togethers, tutorial or workshop-style sessions, community white-paper drafting, or structured debates. Sessions may span multiple days, for instance a data analysis get-together or a paper-drafting exercise that involves "homework" by participants.

There will be two blocks of 90 minutes for splinter sessions each afternoon, separated by a coffee break, with 2 to 3 sessions running in parallel. Splinter sessions run on three of the four conference days (Wednesday afternoon is left free), for a total of 12 to 18 splinter blocks.

Submit a splinter proposal Deadline: 16 November 2026

What a proposal should include

  • Session title
  • Organizer(s) and affiliation(s)
  • A short abstract, a few sentences, describing the session's goals
  • An explicit statement of how the session engages with the "Challenges in Extremely Precise RVs" theme
  • The total number of blocks required (each block is 90 minutes, up to 3 blocks per theme)
  • The intended format: short talks, discussion, hands-on or tutorial, working session, and so on
  • Any prior work required of participants, for example installing software, downloading datasets, or other homework
  • A proposed slate of organizers and participants who commit to attend and participate. Proposers may also solicit abstracts as part of the main abstract submission process.
